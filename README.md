# Riverty Consumer Card — PM Certification Final Project

Individual final project for Product School's Riverty cohort 2, BU Pay & Credit case.

## Project scope

How might Riverty use its first consumer card to build a direct, everyday relationship with users, growing usage and monetisation without undermining its merchant-first strategy? The project covers Germany, from a friends-and-family test launch to a launch recommendation. The scenario is 1,200 invitees over four months (pilot (practice data)); 639 of 1,200 invitees are employees (pilot (practice data)). Treat approval, repayment, and activation rates as likely optimistic because of this sample bias.

**All figures and data in the case pack are fictional practice data, not Riverty facts.** Label every case-data figure in every output as **“pilot (practice data)”**. Do not add confidential Riverty information.

## Working rules

- **Cite evidence.** Every research or data claim must name its source: interview `N-01`–`N-12`, ticket `T-101`–`T-108`, or the CSV filename and column (skip each CSV's first, label row when loading). Do not invent sources or claims.
- **Prefer observed behavior to opinion.** When interview statements conflict with funnel or transaction behavior, state both, favor the behavioral evidence, and list what to check next. Do not average the conflict away.
- **Show the arithmetic.** Give the numerator and denominator for every rate, e.g. `288 / 431 = 66.8% added to wallet`.
- **Stay in phase.** Modules 1–3 describe problems only: no feature, roadmap, or solution proposals. Solutions begin in Module 4 backlog scoring.
- **Respect constraints.** The card platform is shared, capacity is limited across engineering, risk, finance, and operations, and proposition changes need regulatory approval that takes months. Prefer options that avoid re-approval.
- **Apply the merchant-first test.** Every recommendation must say whether it sends spending to Riverty partner merchants or away from them.
- When summarizing research, end with **Contradictions** and **Open questions**.
- Keep writing plain and short; these materials become slides. Do not create an HTML page or document without first proposing its content and receiving explicit approval. Use an existing module template when one applies.

## Case participants and alternatives

- **Lena, 32, Cologne:** heavy BNPL user, mobile-first, no financial cushion; uses pay later so money leaves only after she keeps the item; has Klarna and PayPal and does not prefer Riverty.
- **Merchants (enterprise and SME):** care about conversion, cost of acceptance, and whether the card sends customers to them.
- **Inside Riverty:** engineering, risk, finance, operations, and regulatory stakeholders.
- **Status quo:** a debit card in shops, whichever BNPL option checkout offers, and several apps to track it all.
